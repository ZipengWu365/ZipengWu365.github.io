Research

A common representation framework for time series, sequential learning, and temporal structure.

Current Research Focuses

  1. A theory of temporal representation. Representation operators, their composition, and the properties that connect representation choices to task requirements.
  2. Representations for non-stationary time series. Component decomposition, structural comparison, stationarity-aware retrieval, forecasting, classification, and reproducible evaluation.
  3. Symbolic and learned representations for foundation and world models. Temporal tokenization and abstractions for action histories, VLA trajectories, model rollouts, and temporal memory.

Temporal representation

My research centres on temporal representation, grounded in time-series representation theory. A representation operator maps a time series into a form that exposes useful temporal information. Operators can compose, connecting numerical approximations, components, symbols, features, images, graphs, and learned embeddings.

Representation theory

The framework treats a representation as an operator Φ: T → R and studies how operators compose. Five dimensions organize its analysis: information preservation, distance preservation, invariance, reconstructability, and commutativity. Each task places different demands on these properties. Commutativity concerns whether changing the order of composable operators changes their result.

Composable representation families

  • Vectorial representations: aggregation and approximation (PAA, SAA, PLA, APCA), transform coefficients (DFT, DWT, DCT), and projections (PCA).
  • Component representations: decompositions such as EMD, SSA, and STL expose temporal components.
  • Symbolic representations: SAX, SAA-SAX, SFA, and ABBA encode temporal information as discrete symbols.
  • Statistical and dynamical features: statistical moments, entropy, and recurrence descriptors, including SampEn, permutation entropy (PE), and recurrence quantification analysis (RQA).
  • Image and graph representations: GAF, MTF, recurrence plots (RP), and visibility graphs. STFT and CWT also produce time-frequency maps.
  • Learned embeddings: neural encoders produce latent representations. Foundation and world-model applications extend this framework to broader sequential data.

These families describe how representations are constructed and expressed. They can overlap: an operator may transform numerical coefficients into symbols, or summarize an image as numerical features.

Component representations and decomposition

Decomposition makes temporal components explicit and inspectable. DeTime provides decomposition tooling; TSDecompose-Benchmark evaluates decomposition as a standalone task, alongside the ICML 2026 paper.

Symbolic representation

Discrete descriptions of temporal structure connect time series to token sequences. SAX builds on mean aggregation, while SAA-SAX uses slope aggregation. Both illustrate how numerical and symbolic representation operators compose.

Current projects include Time Series Language and Foundation Model and Language-Action Time-Series Tokenization for Efficient VLA Policies. See the associated compute allocations.

Cross-family goals: compression and denoising

Compression concerns representation size and retained information. It can use aggregation, approximation, coefficient selection, or symbolic encoding. PAA (Piecewise Aggregate Approximation) stores segment means, and SAA (Slopewise Aggregate Approximation) stores segment slopes. Additional quantization and symbolization produce SAX and SAA-SAX, respectively.

Full spectral transforms change the representation; selecting or thresholding coefficients can add compression or denoising. Other goals include interpretability and preserving information relevant to the task. Decomposition and image mappings can expose structure without reducing data size.

Spectral and time-frequency representations

DFT, DCT, and wavelet transforms express temporal information through coefficients. STFT and CWT describe how frequency content changes over time. These operators connect vectorial representations with time-frequency maps and can support periodicity analysis, compression, and denoising.

Comparison operators and relational features

Euclidean distance and DTW compare pairs of sequences. Comparison can also construct representations: Matrix Profile stores local nearest-neighbour distances, and Shapelet Transform represents a sequence by its distances to reference subsequences.

EchoTime compares time series and datasets through explainable structural similarity. Retrieval also connects comparison to forecasting, as in Stationarity-Aware Retrieval-Augmented Time Series Forecasting (KDD 2026).

Forecasting and multi-output prediction

This thread spans taxi demand prediction, hierarchical load forecasting, spatiotemporal prediction, and retrieval-augmented time-series forecasting. Related work on online multi-output regression connects through learning dependencies between outputs.

Classification · current research

Time-series classification extends the representation question to distinguishing classes from temporal structure.

Forecasting, regression, classification, clustering, and anomaly detection can use several representation families. A paper may contribute to an operator, a comparison method, a task, or more than one of these. See the publication list for individual works.
